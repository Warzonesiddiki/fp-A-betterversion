### §1.2 — Day-in-the-life

- **7:00 AM:** Reviews 8 active client engagements; flags 2 with cash-conversion drift.

- **2:00 PM:** Reviews partner-portal report: "are my clients using the tool? Are they happy? Did the rollout succeed?"
- **4:00 PM:** Writes a 1-page memo to the Partner-in-Charge: "Tool X is producing outcomes. Add to recommended list."
- **5:30 PM:** Her incentive comp is tied to (a) team billable hours + (b) outcome quality of her recommendations. Tool failure = reputational cost.

### §1.3 — Pains (5 items, ranked)

1. **"No good answer to the most common question."** Her SaaS clients ask "what FP&A tool should I use?" 30+ times/yr. Today: "stay in Excel" (loses credibility) or "buy Adaptive/Vena/Pigment" (not deeply vetted).
2. **"Can't get case studies from similar-stage CFOs."** Vendors offer case studies from $200M+ ARR customers, not the $30-80M ARR clients Beth serves. She needs peer references.
3. **"The tool I recommend gets blamed when the rollout fails."** Beth's reputation is on the line. She needs low-rollout-failure-rate + vendor co-pilots the rollout.
4. **"My 8 senior managers have 8 different 'outgrew Excel' playbooks."** Beth wants a "Practice Standard" — one tool + one rollout methodology.
5. **"Partner portal uptime directly affects my credibility."** Tool goes down during a client's close cycle → Beth gets the angry call, not the vendor.

### §1.4 — Buying triggers (3 items, ranked)

1. **A SaaS CFO she knows asks "outgrew Excel" question.** Within 1 week, she needs a recommendation. (Happens 30+ times/yr.)
2. **A senior manager asks for a "Practice Standard" tool recommendation.** Beth formalizes this. (2-3 times/yr.)
3. **A peer Practice Lead at a competing firm (RSM, Wipfli, KPMG) recommends a tool.** Competitive pressure. (1-2 times/yr.)

### §1.5 — Tool preferences (5 ranked)

1. **Proven at the $30-80M ARR stage** (her core client segment). Misaligned tools (for $200M+ or $5M) = disqualify.
2. **Excel-compatible formulas** (her clients live in Excel; re-model-from-scratch = non-starter).
3. **Quick rollout (≤30 days contract-to-value)** — her clients won't tolerate 6-mo rollouts.
4. **SOC 2 Type 2 + SOX-ready audit trail** (her clients are public-company-bound or pre-IPO).
5. **A partner portal with client-usage tracking** — she needs visibility into rollout success without polling clients.
